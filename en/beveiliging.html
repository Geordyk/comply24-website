Security and trust

What your procurement and your second line want to see.

Comply24 is an ICT service provider to supervised institutions. So you assess us the way you assess any ICT service provider: on where the data sits, on access, on the audit trail, on exit and on what the contract says. This page sets out what we commit to and what we hand over.

What we commit to

Five commitments you can test.

Implemented

Your own environment

Every institution gets its own tenant. Hosting is in the European Union.

Separation between clients is enforced at database level, not in the application code. The boundary therefore sits in the database and not in the screen logic.

Development, acceptance and production are separate environments. Your documents live in production only.

Implemented

Access and audit trail

Access runs through Microsoft Entra ID. Roles come from your own groups, so MFA and conditional access stay with you.

Every step and every change is in the audit trail, with name and time. The audit trail is append-only: entries are never edited or deleted.

Four-eyes is identity-based. Whoever drafts something cannot approve it. There is no override, not even for administrators.

Implemented

AI in Europe

The model is hosted in Europe and is not trained on your data.

Every outcome is logged with name and time: what the model proposed, the reasoning behind it, and who confirmed, corrected or rejected it.

The default works without an external model. Scoring, prioritisation and coverage follow rules you can read.

Implemented

Ownership and exit

All data is yours. Export to Excel and Word, at any moment, without our involvement.

Escrow on request.

On termination you keep the outcomes; the environment is closed or kept to go on tracking changes. What happens to your documents is set out in the contract annex.

In preparation

Certification and testing

ISO 27001 is on the roadmap.

The platform has been penetration tested.

We share the security documentation under NDA: the security design, the penetration test report and the list of subprocessors.

Vendor file

What DORA Article 30 asks of your contract, and what we provide for it.

Article 30(2) of DORA lists what a contract with an ICT service provider must contain. Below is what you get from us for each item. The underlying documentation is sent under NDA.

What DORA requiresWhat Comply24 provides
30(2)(a)Description of the service and of subcontractingA service description per product, with the list of subprocessors and a duty to notify on change.
30(2)(b)Locations of processing and storageSet out in the contract annex, with advance notice of any intended change.
30(2)(c)Availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentialityThe security design, under NDA, with the status of each measure.
30(2)(d)Access, recovery and return of dataExport to Excel and Word at any moment; escrow on request; the exit window in the contract annex.
30(2)(e)Service levelsTurnaround times and availability in the contract annex, with the revision procedure.
30(2)(f)Assistance on ICT incidentsNotification chain and contact path, with the window within which we notify you.
30(2)(g)Cooperation with authoritiesAudit and inspection rights, and cooperation with your supervisor, in the contract annex.
30(2)(h)Termination and notice periodsTermination rights and minimum notice periods in the contract annex.

Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, Article 30(2). The annex follows the text of the regulation; your counsel reads it alongside your own outsourcing policy.
